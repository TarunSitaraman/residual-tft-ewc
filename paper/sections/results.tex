\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\emph{Data.} S1 diagnostics track the current SOH (median within-cell $\rho=0.97$ for the main incremental-capacity peak) but not the change \emph{to the next RPT} (median $|\rho|\le0.3$), and every S1 sample stays in class C0. S6 conditions fade very differently, and all cells cross from C0 to C3.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Continual stream (P1)}
\label{sec:res-p1}

\input{tables/p1}

Table~\ref{tab:p1} gives the final average error, skill and forgetting.

\emph{S1.} TFT\,+\,EWC reaches 0.462~pp (skill 0.27), level with gradient boosting (0.466~pp), TFT fine-tuning (0.467~pp) and MLP replay (0.475~pp); a ridge regression on the planned usage alone is slightly better (0.450~pp). Forgetting is small for every method (0.016--0.040~pp). Persistence and linear extrapolation degrade quickly with the horizon (0.83 and 0.90~pp at $h{=}3$), while the learned models stay between 0.42 and 0.49~pp. Comparisons with TFT\,+\,EWC whose significance changed in a re-run differ by only 0.005--0.014~pp, an order of magnitude below the label shift of the pulse-charge definition (\S\ref{sec:res-repro}); we therefore report S1 method differences as practically equivalent.

\emph{S6.} When tasks differ in usage, forgetting is substantial (0.18--0.46~pp). EWC lowers the TFT's mean error and mean forgetting relative to fine-tuning (0.677 against 0.883~pp), but which of the two differences is significant changed between runs (\S\ref{sec:res-repro}), so we do not claim a significant benefit. TFT\,+\,EWC is not the most accurate method: MLP fine-tuning (0.531~pp) and MLP\,+\,EWC (0.540~pp) are significantly better in both runs, the joint upper bounds (0.414 and 0.427~pp) put the TFT's continual-learning cost at about 0.25~pp, and linear extrapolation of the recent fade rate (0.343~pp) and gradient boosting (0.372~pp) are the best forecasters. The TFT varies strongly with task order (s.d.\ 0.26~pp with EWC, 0.54~pp with fine-tuning), and the diagnostic features do not help it (0.621~pp without them).

\subsection{Unseen cell or usage condition (P2)}
\label{sec:res-p2}

\input{tables/p2}

Gradient boosting is best on both datasets (Table~\ref{tab:p2}). On S1 the MLP, the TFT and the usage-only ridge are close (0.285--0.301~pp); on S6 the 100\% depth-of-discharge condition is the hardest for every method (1.8--2.4~pp). The reuse class of the forecast SOH agrees with the measured class for 95--97\% of S6 samples with the learned models (macro-F1 0.83--0.88), against 85\% (0.64) for persistence. With six or seven held-out groups, no difference with the TFT survives Holm correction.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Reproducibility of the comparisons}
\label{sec:res-repro}

P1 and P2 were re-run in a pinned environment on data rebuilt from the raw archives and verified against recorded checksums. Every baseline, both joint upper bounds, every P2 result and the ranking of methods reproduced; the neural models moved by at most 0.059~pp (TFT\,+\,EWC on S6: 0.677 to 0.704~pp). On S1, five Holm-corrected comparisons with TFT\,+\,EWC crossed $p=0.05$. On S6, validation selected EWC $\lambda=1000$ instead of 100, a 0.008~pp tie over three tuning runs; the error advantage of TFT\,+\,EWC over TFT fine-tuning then moved from $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.005$ to 0.060, while its reduction of forgetting moved from 0.050 to 0.008. These findings motivated the ten tuning orders, the one-standard-error rule and the replication rule used for P3.

\subsection{First-life pretraining (P3)}
\label{sec:res-p3}

The pool provides 36{,}711 samples, against 2{,}409 in S6. Validation selected EWC $\lambda=1000$ for the MLP but only 0.01 for the pretrained TFT. Under the replication rule (Fig.~\ref{fig:p3}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item EWC and fine-tuning are practically equivalent for the pretrained TFT in final error, forgetting and backward transfer (differences of 0.015--0.017~pp in both seed sets);
  \item pretrained TFT\,+\,EWC beats pretrained MLP fine-tuning (by 0.334 and 0.185~pp), also in forgetting and backward transfer;
  \item both backbones are better from scratch on average (TFT 0.401/0.415~pp against 0.509/0.591~pp pretrained with EWC), significant for the TFT only in the replicate set;
  \item linear extrapolation (0.343~pp) remains the most accurate method.
\end{itemize}
The S6 stream does not degrade the pool test error (TFT\,+\,EWC: 3.280 to 3.148~pp), but on that split the pretrained models are less accurate than linear extrapolation (3.001~pp): with the shared inputs, pretraining does not learn a first-life model worth transferring. Pretraining takes 22~s (MLP) and 251~s (TFT) on one CPU thread; EWC adds about 50\% to the TFT's stream time without improving accuracy.

\emph{Why the TFT improved.} A post hoc ablation, repeated on the replicate seeds (Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation}), explains why the TFT trained from scratch improves from 0.891~pp with the earlier inputs to 0.401~pp with the shared inputs: dropping the 17 diagnostic features lowers the error by 0.17--0.35~pp and adding the calendar time between checks by 0.19--0.31~pp, both replicated at both learning rates ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}\le0.002$). The learning rate has no significant effect in either seed set.

\emph{What the TFT uses.} Its top-weighted input changes between seeds, inputs constant within S6 receive weight, and its attention over the four previous origins is nearly uniform (0.24--0.26 per lag): the weights are not a reliable measure of importance here, and the TFT makes little use of its history.

Table~\ref{tab:residual} and Fig.~\ref{fig:residual} give the results of the residual form~\eqref{eq:residual}. The criterion fixed in advance for a positive result, residual TFT\,+\,EWC beating linear extrapolation, was \emph{not} met: the two are practically equivalent for horizons 1--3 (differences of $-0.004$ and $-0.029$~pp), and for horizons 1--6 extrapolation is better on average ($+0.076$ and $+0.049$~pp, not replicated). Per horizon, extrapolation is better at $h=1$ and 2 (replicated), and the gap closes without reversing by $h=5$--6. Learning the residual is, however, what makes the TFT competitive: it improves TFT\,+\,EWC over the direct form by 0.17~pp (E1) and 0.24--0.30~pp (E2), and puts it ahead of gradient boosting, all replicated; it is the most accurate of the TFT and MLP variants. With horizons 1--6, where the residual TFT forgets more, EWC lowers its forgetting from 0.28/0.22 to 0.09~pp and its backward transfer from 0.18/0.14 to 0.03~pp, replicated; its effect on the final error is not. For horizons 1--3, EWC and fine-tuning are practically equivalent.

\emph{Ageing-cycle inputs.} Adding the 21 Spark-extracted inputs (Section~\ref{sec:spark}) to the residual TFT\,+\,EWC, as specified before the run, makes it worse: 0.480/0.501 against 0.339/0.314~pp, and worse than linear extrapolation, both replicated; gradient boosting also worsens (0.495 against 0.451~pp).

\subsection{Shift-adaptive EWC and literature baselines}
\label{sec:res-d24}

Table~\ref{tab:residual} also gives the second pre-registered experiment. The criterion for SA-EWC was \emph{not} met: RC-TFT and the fixed-$\lambda$ residual TFT\,+\,EWC are practically equivalent in final error and in forgetting for both horizon sets (differences of at most 0.03~pp), although the shift weights did vary (0.05--1.14). As fixed in advance, the simpler fixed-$\lambda$ model therefore remains the best-supported variant. Like it, RC-TFT is level with extrapolation for horizons 1--3 and not better for 1--6. The residual TFT is not uniquely accurate at short horizons: BiLSTM\,+\,EWC (direct and residual) and the residual Transformer\,+\,EWC are practically equivalent to RC-TFT for horizons 1--3. For horizons 1--6, RC-TFT beats the residual BiLSTM and TCN (replicated; the Transformer difference is not replicated). Every single-fit regressor is worse than RC-TFT, replicated, except the residual SVR for horizons 1--6.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Shrinkage and intervals on the stream}
\label{sec:res-d25}

Shrinking the correction per horizon makes the residual TFT\,+\,EWC practically equivalent to extrapolation at every horizon (0.318/0.308 against 0.343~pp for horizons 1--3, 0.498/0.493 against 0.485~pp for 1--6), removing its replicated loss at $h=1$--2; the correction is turned down most at $h=1$ (median $\alpha_1=0.28$ for 1--6). This criterion for a positive result was again not met: the model matches extrapolation, it does not beat it. Its 90\% intervals are narrower than extrapolation's but under-cover (0.71--0.82, against 0.92--0.95), so the interval scores are equal (practically equivalent or not replicated). Pretraining the residual model on the first-life pool makes it worse for horizons 1--3 (by 0.22~pp, replicated) and does not help for 1--6.

Table~\ref{tab:d26} gives the fourth experiment. As registered, the shrunk residual TFT\,+\,EWC beats extrapolation at origins with a fade rate, by 0.12/0.12~pp for horizons 1--3 and 0.20/0.18~pp for 1--6 (replicated): the first comparison in which the proposed model beats extrapolation. The learning rate and $\lambda$ come from P1 tuning streams that used validation rows of all seven conditions, which can only favour the TFT.

\emph{Intervals on unseen conditions.} Table~\ref{tab:d27} combines the fourth and fifth experiments. Intervals calibrated on the pooled validation rows of the training conditions under-cover on the held-out one, for the TFT and for extrapolation alike. Calibrating extrapolation, which has no fitted parameters, on all origins of the same conditions instead of their mid-life validation rows restores 0.89--0.90: the calibration rows, not only the model, caused the shortfall. With leave-one-condition-out calibration, gradient-boosting intervals reach the registered threshold of 0.85 (0.90 in both horizon sets) with the lowest interval scores, lower than calibrated extrapolation's on six of seven conditions (significant for horizons 1--3, $p=0.047$, but not for 1--6, $p=0.30$, so not established). The coverage holds over conditions, not for each: on the 100\% depth-of-discharge condition, which lies outside the range of the others, it is 0.48 and 0.38. Per-stratum half-widths raise extrapolation's coverage at origins without a fade rate from 0.01--0.02 to 0.91--0.92 and change little for gradient boosting. With these intervals, 87--92\% of held-out forecasts lie within one reuse class, and 99.4--99.7\% of those classes are correct.
