\section{Related Work}

We review studies from 2022--2026 (Table~\ref{tab:literature}; review in~\cite{ref6}). Second-life studies estimate the \emph{current} SOH of a fixed population from partial charging under residential and fast-charging profiles (RMSE about 0.6 and 0.8\%, with 85\% less testing time than a capacity test)~\cite{ref1}, from 58 health indicators of reused Nissan Leaf modules (about 0.2\% RMSE, though the most useful indicators changed with ageing)~\cite{ref2}, from impedance spectra~\cite{ref3}, with bagged Gaussian process regression for screening (worst-case error below 1.48\%)~\cite{ref4}, and online with clustering for Leaf cells aged over 15 months (error below 2.3\%)~\cite{ref5}. None adapts a model as new populations arrive.

Continual learning has predicted capacity degradation~\cite{ref7} and, with progressive networks and TCNs, the state of charge across drive cycles, addressing forgetting and task order~\cite{ref8}. Closest to our setting, pulse-test conditions have been learned as domain-incremental tasks with a replay buffer to estimate second-life SOH with near-zero forgetting~\cite{tao2026pcl}. Domain generalisation~\cite{ref9} and probabilistic BiLSTM/TCN models with isotonic recalibration~\cite{ref10} target second-life SOH without a task sequence.

\emph{Research gap.} Continual learning for second-life SOH has so far targeted \emph{estimation} of the current SOH~\cite{tao2026pcl}. To our knowledge, it has not been evaluated for \emph{forecasting} across heterogeneous second-life populations with reuse screening, measured forgetting and comparison with simple forecasters and joint training. We ask whether a continual learner can learn such populations in sequence while retaining earlier ones, and whether its forecasts support reuse screening.
