\section{Related Work}

The review covers 2022--2026 studies (Table~\ref{tab:literature}; review in~\cite{ref6}). Second-life SOH has been estimated from partial charging under residential and fast-charging profiles (RMSE about 0.6 and 0.8\%, 85\% less testing time than a capacity test)~\cite{ref1}, from 58 health indicators of reused Nissan Leaf modules (about 0.2\% RMSE, but the most useful indicators changed with ageing)~\cite{ref2}, from impedance spectra~\cite{ref3}, and with bagged Gaussian process regression for screening (worst-case error below 1.48\%)~\cite{ref4}; retired Leaf cells aged for over 15 months were tracked online with clustering (error below 2.3\%)~\cite{ref5}. These studies estimate the \emph{current} SOH of a fixed population; none adapts a model as new populations arrive. Continual learning has predicted capacity degradation~\cite{ref7} and, with progressive networks and TCNs, the state of charge across drive cycles, addressing forgetting and task order~\cite{ref8}; domain generalisation~\cite{ref9} and probabilistic BiLSTM/TCN models, whose calibration error isotonic recalibration reduced~\cite{ref10}, target second-life SOH but not a long sequence of tasks with explicit retention.

\emph{Research gap.} Second-life SOH estimation, screening, transfer learning and degradation prediction have progressed largely separately; continual learning is rarely combined with heterogeneous second-life SOH forecasting, an explicit reuse decision and an evaluation of forgetting. This work asks whether a continual learner can learn heterogeneous second-life populations in sequence while retaining earlier ones, and whether its forecasts support reuse decisions.
