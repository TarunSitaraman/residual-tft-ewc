\section{Related Work}

The review covers 2022--2026 studies on second-life lithium-ion batteries, SOH estimation, continual learning and reuse assessment (Table~\ref{tab:literature}).

\emph{SOH estimation for second life.} Braco \textit{et al.} estimated the SOH of second-life cells from partial charging under residential and fast-charging-station profiles, with offline RMSE of about 0.6 and 0.8\% and 85\% less testing time than a full capacity test~\cite{ref1}. In a companion study on six reused Nissan Leaf modules they compared 58 health indicators from incremental-capacity analysis, partial charging, constant-current/constant-voltage stages and internal resistance; the best indicator at repurposing reached about 0.2\% RMSE, but the most useful indicators changed as ageing progressed~\cite{ref2}. Faraji-Niri \textit{et al.} accelerated second-life SOH estimation with electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) and machine learning~\cite{ref3}; EIS is fast and non-destructive but sensitive to temperature, state of charge and equipment. Takahashi \textit{et al.} screened batteries for second life with statistical current and voltage features and bagged Gaussian process regression, with root-mean-squared percent error below 1.48\% in the worst cases~\cite{ref4}. Cui \textit{et al.} tested retired Nissan Leaf cells for more than 15 months under grid-storage-like operation and estimated health online with clustering, with mean absolute percentage error below 2.3\%~\cite{ref5}. Vignesh \textit{et al.} reviewed SOH estimation methods for second-life batteries and their challenges~\cite{ref6}. These studies estimate the \emph{current} SOH of a fixed population; none adapts a model as new, different populations arrive.

\emph{Continual learning and generalisation.} Lee \textit{et al.} applied bilevel-optimised continual learning to predict the capacity degradation of lithium-ion batteries~\cite{ref7}. Yao \textit{et al.} used progressive neural networks with temporal convolutional networks for online state-of-charge estimation across three public drive-cycle datasets, explicitly addressing catastrophic forgetting and task order~\cite{ref8}; the target, however, is state of charge, not SOH or reuse. Li \textit{et al.} proposed domain-generalised SOH estimation for second-life EV batteries~\cite{ref9}, and Meskine \textit{et al.} compared BiLSTM, TCN and probabilistic TCN-GPR models on sparse, noisy data, where isotonic recalibration substantially reduced the calibration error~\cite{ref10}. Transfer and domain generalisation do not by themselves address a long sequence of tasks with explicit retention.

\emph{Research gap.} Second-life SOH estimation, fast screening, transfer learning and degradation prediction have progressed largely separately. Continual learning offers a way to learn new battery populations while retaining earlier ones, but it is rarely combined with heterogeneous second-life SOH forecasting, an explicit reuse decision and an evaluation of forgetting, and the cost of continual updates is seldom weighed against accuracy. This work asks whether a continual learner can progressively learn heterogeneous second-life populations while retaining earlier ones, and whether its forecasts support reuse decisions.
