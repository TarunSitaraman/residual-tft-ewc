\section{Related Work}

Second-life studies (review:~\cite{ref6}) estimate the current SOH of a fixed set of batteries from partial charging~\cite{ref1}, health indicators~\cite{ref2}, impedance spectra~\cite{ref3}, bagged Gaussian process regression (GPR)~\cite{ref4} or online clustering~\cite{ref5}, with errors up to about 2.3\%; none updates its model as new groups arrive. Continual learning has predicted capacity loss~\cite{ref7} and state of charge across drive cycles~\cite{ref8}, and, closest to us, estimated second-life SOH across pulse-test conditions with replay~\cite{tao2026pcl}. Domain generalisation~\cite{ref9} and probabilistic bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM) and temporal convolutional network (TCN) models~\cite{ref10} target second-life SOH without a task sequence. \textbf{Gap:} continual forecasting across heterogeneous second-life groups, with forgetting measured, reuse screening and comparison against simple forecasters and joint training, has not been tested.
