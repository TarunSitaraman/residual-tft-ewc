\section{Introduction}

Lithium-ion batteries lose capacity and power with cycling, temperature and time. When an electric-vehicle (EV) battery no longer meets automotive requirements, it may still serve less demanding applications such as stationary storage, renewable-energy buffering or backup power. Reuse extends the battery's life and delays recycling, but only if its health is assessed reliably: a battery that suits one application may not suit another.

The state of health (SOH) describes the present capability of a battery relative to a reference condition, and machine-learning models have estimated it from voltage, current, temperature, partial charging and impedance measurements~\cite{ref1,ref2,ref3,ref4}. Retired batteries are, however, heterogeneous: chemistry, manufacturer, capacity, first-life history, temperature and charging protocol differ, and complete first-life records are often missing. A model trained once on a fixed population may therefore fail on the next population. Continual learning addresses this by updating a model on new data while preserving what it learned earlier; it must therefore be evaluated both on the newly introduced battery group and on the groups learned before, to measure catastrophic forgetting.

This work combines continual learning, health assessment and second-life reuse assessment. Health is assessed by \emph{forecasting} the SOH one to three capacity checks ahead from everything measured up to the latest check: on the first dataset studied, estimating the current SOH from cycling features was beaten by carrying the last measured SOH forward (\S\ref{sec:methods}), whereas a reuse decision needs the future SOH. The forecast is mapped to reuse-suitability classes.

The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A leakage-controlled benchmark for forecasting the SOH of second-life cells, built from five public datasets (395 cells after quality rules) verified against their repository records, with automated data checks and protocols fixed before model training.
  \item Two label artefacts identified and removed: test currents set from the previous capacity, which encode the label, and pulse charge counted in the capacity, which shifts labels by up to 0.09 percentage points.
  \item An evaluation of a Temporal Fusion Transformer with Elastic Weight Consolidation (TFT\,+\,EWC) against fine-tuning, replay, joint training, simple forecasters and the model families of the literature (BiLSTM, TCN, Transformer, GPR, random forest, SVR) under three protocols (a continual stream, unseen groups and first-life pretraining), reporting adaptation, forgetting and retention.
  \item A continual TFT\,+\,EWC that learns the correction to linear extrapolation: it is among the most accurate learned forecasters on the S6 stream, level with extrapolation for horizons 1--3, and its EWC reduces forgetting over longer horizons (replicated); a pre-registered shift-adaptive EWC (RC-TFT) is practically equivalent to it, a null result we report.
  \item A reproducibility analysis showing that several significance results on small battery datasets depend on the numerical environment, and a replication rule that the main results satisfy.
\end{itemize}
