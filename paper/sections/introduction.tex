\section{Introduction}

An electric-vehicle (EV) battery that no longer meets automotive requirements can often still serve as stationary storage or backup power. Reuse extends its life and delays recycling, but only if its health is judged reliably.

The state of health (SOH) describes a battery's present capability relative to a reference condition. Machine-learning models have estimated it from voltage, current, temperature, partial-charging and impedance measurements~\cite{ref1,ref2,ref3,ref4}. Retired batteries, however, differ in chemistry, manufacturer, capacity, first-life history, temperature and charging protocol, and their first-life records are often incomplete, so a model trained once may fail on the next population. Continual learning updates a model on new data while preserving earlier knowledge, and must be tested on old and new groups alike to reveal forgetting.

This work combines continual learning with health forecasting and a first screening step for reuse. We \emph{forecast} the SOH one to six capacity checks ahead from everything measured up to the latest check: on the first dataset, estimating the current SOH from cycling features was beaten by simply carrying the last measured SOH forward (\S\ref{sec:methods}), and a reuse decision depends on future, not only present, health. The forecast is mapped to capacity-based classes that support, but do not replace, a full reuse assessment.

Our contributions are as follows (Fig.~\ref{fig:system}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item A leakage-controlled benchmark for forecasting the SOH of second-life cells from five public datasets (395 cells after quality rules), with two label artefacts removed: test currents set from the previous capacity, and pulse charge counted in the capacity.
  \item An evaluation of a Temporal Fusion Transformer with Elastic Weight Consolidation (TFT\,+\,EWC) against fine-tuning, replay, joint training, simple forecasters and the model families used in the literature (BiLSTM, TCN, Transformer, GPR, random forest, SVR) under three protocols (a continual stream, unseen groups, first-life pretraining), reporting adaptation, forgetting and retention.
  \item Residual TFT-EWC, a continual learner that corrects linear extrapolation rather than replacing it, and the only model competitive in both settings: on the stream it matches extrapolation, which no model beats, outperforms gradient boosting and forgets about two thirds less with EWC; on unseen usage conditions it outperforms extrapolation and comes within 0.08~pp of gradient boosting held to the same no-storage constraint (replicated).
  \item Forecast intervals for unseen usage conditions: calibrated on the training conditions they cover too little, whereas calibrating on conditions held out from fitting restores nominal coverage, except for a condition outside the training range.
  \item A reproducibility analysis: several significance results on small battery datasets depend on the numerical environment, so the main results must pass a replication rule.
\end{itemize}
