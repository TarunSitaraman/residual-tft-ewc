\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{Why the proposed model does not win.} On the continual stream TFT\,+\,EWC never beat linear extrapolation: fade over one to three checks is smooth, leaving little structure for a sequence model to add; the groups are few and small (21--27 and 135--225 training samples per task in S1 and S6); and the TFT barely uses its history. EWC behaves as expected where there is something to protect (forgetting is negligible across the near-identical S1 cells and substantial across the S6 conditions), and its clearest benefit came once the TFT learned only the correction to extrapolation, cutting forgetting over six horizons by about two thirds. Making the penalty follow the measured shift added nothing. The backbone matters less than the target: at horizons 1--3 a BiLSTM or Transformer with EWC is as accurate as the residual TFT. On unseen conditions the continual constraint itself becomes the main cost: trained jointly, the same model is 0.32--0.44~pp more accurate. A model never shown the new condition gains nothing from adapting and still pays for the order of the stream; with datasets this small, storing every earlier group is cheap, so the constraint that motivates continual learning did not bind.

\emph{What matters.} Building on the best simple forecaster mattered most, and the ablation points to the inputs rather than the architecture: the diagnostic features hurt the TFT, and the calendar time between checks helped, consistent with fade per cycle rising with that time at 50~$^\circ$C in all 20 S6 cells. For intervals, the calibration rows mattered as much as the model: residuals from mid-life validation origins of conditions seen in training understate the error on a new condition, while residuals from conditions held out from fitting reproduce it. With few cells per condition, 21 dense ageing-cycle inputs added more variance than information, and first-life cells of other chemistries did not transfer to the LFP cells of S6.

\emph{Reproducibility.} Several significance results changed in a re-run on different hardware (Section~\ref{sec:res-repro}). We recommend recording the full software environment, tuning with enough runs to separate neighbouring grid values, and requiring that claimed differences replicate on a second seed set and exceed a practically meaningful threshold.

\emph{Reuse and positioning.} The reuse-class mapping is informative only when the horizon crosses class boundaries: S1 stays in C0 and the S6 stream's test periods lie in C3. For a battery population unlike any seen before, such as the 100\% depth-of-discharge condition here, neither the forecasts nor the intervals can be trusted, and pooled coverage does not reveal it. The studies of Section~II estimate the current SOH or SOC on other cells and with other SOH definitions, so their errors are not directly comparable with ours.

\emph{Limitations.} The task was changed to forecasting after the first dataset was inspected; the second-life datasets are small; S6 is aged at 50~$^\circ$C and its ``second life'' continues the same protocol; SOH is relative to each cell's first valid measurement; forecasts are conditional on planned usage; and the ablation and every later experiment were decided after the main results (all except the ablation were specified and committed before they were run). The single-fit regressors see all tasks at once, and the direct literature backbones were run for horizons 1--3 only. The TFT hyperparameters used on unseen conditions were tuned with validation rows of all seven S6 conditions, and the unseen-condition results rest on seven conditions from one laboratory, with the conditions fixed and only training randomness varied. Leave-one-condition-out intervals were run for gradient boosting and extrapolation only.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We evaluated a Temporal Fusion Transformer with Elastic Weight Consolidation as a continual learner for forecasting the SOH of second-life cells, on a leakage-controlled benchmark with pre-registered follow-up experiments and replicated differences. Learning a shrunk correction to linear extrapolation made it as accurate as extrapolation on the continual stream and more accurate on unseen usage conditions, where, however, gradient boosting and the same TFT trained jointly were clearly better: at this data scale, learning continually cost more than it gave. Conformal intervals held on unseen conditions only when calibrated on conditions held out from fitting, and not for a condition outside the training range. Refitting and tracking the width as checks arrive cut the interval score by 29\%; with these intervals, 87--93\% of forecasts fell within one reuse class, over 99\% of those correctly. Future work should test continual learning where earlier data cannot be stored and populations are larger and more varied, and should calibrate intervals across laboratories and chemistries.
