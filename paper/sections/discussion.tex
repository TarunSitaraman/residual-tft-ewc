\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{Why it works.} Building on the best simple forecaster mattered most: extrapolation fails on new conditions, gradient boosting forgets on the stream, and the residual model inherits extrapolation's stability while learning condition effects (Figs.~\ref{fig:overview}, \ref{fig:dash-s6}a,b). Most of the remaining gap to GB-ALL and joint training comes from storing data, not from the model: with 40 rows per condition, V2 matches joint training. Continual learning therefore suits data that cannot be kept (privacy, ownership, volume).

\emph{Reuse.} The classes screen on relative capacity only; power, safety, absolute capacity and cost also matter. For a group unlike any seen before (condition~7, Figs.~\ref{fig:dash-s6}d, \ref{fig:dash-unseen}a), neither forecasts nor intervals can be trusted, and pooled coverage hides it.

\emph{Limitations.} We switched to forecasting after seeing S1, and all follow-ups came after the main results (registered except the ablation; the GB stream test and V2 are post hoc, and S7/S10 were added to find a setting where the model wins). Second-life sets are small, S6 is aged at 50~$^\circ$C, SOH is relative to the first check, and planned usage is assumed known.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Residual TFT-EWC forecasts second-life SOH as a shrunk correction to linear extrapolation and is the only model compared that holds up both on a continual stream and on unseen conditions, including all 64 S10 conditions against gradient boosting under the same no-storage rule. Models that store all earlier data remain more accurate; a small replay memory closes most of that gap. Intervals hold only when calibrated on held-out conditions within the training range. Future work: larger groups whose data cannot be stored, and power and safety criteria for screening.
