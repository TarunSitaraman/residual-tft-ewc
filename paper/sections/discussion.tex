\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{Where the proposed model is strongest.} Its advantage is robustness: extrapolation is accurate on the stream but degrades on new conditions, and gradient boosting is accurate on new conditions but trails on the stream, whereas Residual TFT-EWC is competitive in both. On the stream, EWC protects earlier groups where there is something to protect (S6, not S1), cutting forgetting over six horizons by about two thirds. Matching extrapolation is itself demanding: fade is smooth and the groups are small (21--27 and 135--225 training samples per task in S1 and S6). On unseen conditions, the remaining gap to gradient boosting and to joint training (0.32--0.44~pp) comes mainly from storing earlier data rather than from the model: under the same no-storage constraint, gradient boosting is within 0.08~pp. Where earlier data cannot be kept, for privacy, ownership or volume, continual learning is the natural choice; in our small datasets storing every group was still cheap. A shift-adaptive penalty added nothing.

\emph{What drives its accuracy.} Building on the best simple forecaster mattered most; the ablation points to inputs, not architecture: diagnostic features hurt the TFT, and the calendar time between checks helped, consistent with fade per cycle rising with that time at 50~$^\circ$C in all 20 S6 cells. For intervals, the calibration rows mattered as much as the model: only residuals from held-out conditions reproduce the error on a new one.

\emph{Reproducibility and reuse.} As significance changed across platforms (Section~\ref{sec:res-repro}), we recommend requiring replicated differences above a practical threshold. The reuse classes are a screening aid, not a suitability verdict: they rest on relative capacity alone, whereas reuse also depends on power capability, safety, absolute capacity and cost. For a population unlike any seen, such as the 100\% depth-of-discharge condition, neither forecasts nor intervals can be trusted, which pooled coverage hides.

\emph{Limitations.} The task was changed to forecasting after the first dataset was inspected; the second-life datasets are small; S6 is aged at 50~$^\circ$C and its ``second life'' continues the same protocol; SOH is relative to each cell's first valid measurement; forecasts are conditional on planned usage; and the ablation and every later experiment were decided after the main results (all but the ablation committed before being run). The single-fit regressors see all tasks at once, and the direct literature backbones were run for horizons 1--3 only. The TFT hyperparameters for unseen conditions were tuned with validation rows of all seven S6 conditions, all from one laboratory. Leave-one-condition-out intervals were run for gradient boosting and extrapolation only.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We proposed Residual TFT-EWC, a continual learner for forecasting the SOH of second-life cells that learns a shrunk correction to linear extrapolation. It is the only model compared that stays competitive across settings: on the stream it matches extrapolation, outperforms gradient boosting and forgets far less with EWC, and on unseen usage conditions it outperforms extrapolation. Gradient boosting and joint training are more accurate on unseen conditions mainly because they store every earlier condition's data; without that, gradient boosting is within 0.08~pp. Conformal intervals held on unseen conditions only when calibrated on conditions held out from fitting, and not outside the training range; refitting and tracking cut the interval score by 29\%. Future work should test it where earlier data cannot be stored and populations are larger, and add power and safety criteria to the screening.
