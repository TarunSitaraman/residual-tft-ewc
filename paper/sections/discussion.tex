\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{Why the proposed model does not win.} TFT\,+\,EWC never beat linear extrapolation. Fade over one to three checks is smooth, so extrapolating the recent fade rate leaves little structure for a sequence model to add; the groups are few and small (21--27 and 135--225 training samples per task in S1 and S6); and the TFT barely uses its history window. EWC behaves as expected where there is something to protect: forgetting is negligible across the near-identical S1 cells and substantial across the S6 usage conditions. Even there its benefit to the direct TFT did not survive a re-run, and after pretraining validation selected almost no EWC. Its clearest benefit came once the TFT learned only the correction to extrapolation: over six horizons, EWC cut that model's forgetting by about two thirds, replicated.

\emph{What matters.} Building on the best simple forecaster mattered most: the learned residual made TFT\,+\,EWC the most accurate learned model on the S6 stream and level with extrapolation for horizons 1--3. The ablation also points to the inputs rather than the architecture: the diagnostic features hurt the TFT, and the calendar time between checks helped, consistent with fade per cycle rising with that time at 50~$^\circ$C in all 20 S6 cells. Usage known in advance is a strong predictor, and forecasts are conditional on it. Dense measurements between checks did not help: with few cells per condition, 21 extra inputs added more variance than information. Pretraining on 369 first-life cells of other chemistries did not transfer to the LFP cells of S6, although retention of the pool was not the problem.

\emph{Reproducibility.} Several significance results changed in a re-run on different hardware (Section~\ref{sec:res-repro}); small datasets, many low-variance runs and Holm-corrected tests make such fragility likely. We recommend recording the full software environment, tuning with enough runs to separate neighbouring grid values, and requiring that claimed differences replicate on a second seed set and exceed a practically meaningful threshold.

\emph{Reuse and positioning.} The reuse-class mapping is informative only when the forecast horizon crosses class boundaries: S1 stays in C0 and the S6 stream's test periods lie in C3, while on unseen S6 conditions the classes agree for 95--97\% of samples with the learned models. The studies of Section~II estimate the current SOH or SOC on other cells and with other SOH definitions, so their errors are not directly comparable with ours.

\emph{Limitations.} The task was changed to forecasting after the first dataset was inspected; the second-life datasets are small; S6 is aged at 50~$^\circ$C and its ``second life'' continues the same protocol; SOH is relative to each cell's first valid measurement; forecasts are conditional on planned usage; and the ablation and the residual experiment were decided after the main results (the latter was specified and committed before it was run).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We built a leakage-controlled benchmark for forecasting the SOH of second-life lithium-ion cells from five public datasets and evaluated a Temporal Fusion Transformer with Elastic Weight Consolidation as a continual learner. Learned forecasters beat persistence clearly, and forgetting appears whenever usage conditions differ. Learning the correction to linear extrapolation made TFT\,+\,EWC the most accurate learned model on the S6 stream, 0.17--0.30~pp better than its direct form and level with extrapolation for horizons 1--3, and with this form EWC reduced forgetting over longer horizons; all of these replicated on a second seed set. No model, however, beat extrapolation of the recent fade rate, first-life pretraining on other chemistries did not transfer, and inputs mattered more than architecture. Future work should test continual learning on larger and more varied second-life populations, use the dense per-cycle data between checks, and quantify forecast uncertainty near reuse-class boundaries.
