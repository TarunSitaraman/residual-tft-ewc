\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{Why the proposed model does not win.} On the stream, TFT\,+\,EWC never beat linear extrapolation: fade is smooth, the groups are few and small (21--27 and 135--225 training samples per task in S1 and S6), and the TFT's attention over its history is nearly uniform. EWC helps where there is something to protect (S6, not S1), most clearly on the residual target, where it cuts forgetting over six horizons by about two thirds; a shift-adaptive penalty added nothing, and at horizons 1--3 a BiLSTM or Transformer with EWC is as accurate. On unseen conditions the continual constraint itself is the main cost (joint training is 0.32--0.44~pp more accurate). With data this small, storing every earlier group is cheap, so the constraint that motivates continual learning did not bind.

\emph{What matters.} Building on the best simple forecaster mattered most; the ablation points to inputs, not architecture: diagnostic features hurt the TFT, and the calendar time between checks helped, consistent with fade per cycle rising with that time at 50~$^\circ$C in all 20 S6 cells. For intervals, the calibration rows mattered as much as the model: only residuals from held-out conditions reproduce the error on a new one.

\emph{Reproducibility and reuse.} As significance changed across platforms (Section~\ref{sec:res-repro}), we recommend recording the environment and requiring replicated differences above a practical threshold. The reuse classes are a screening aid, not a suitability verdict: they rest on relative capacity alone, whereas reuse also depends on power capability, safety, absolute capacity and cost. They are informative only when the horizon crosses class boundaries, and for a population unlike any seen, such as the 100\% depth-of-discharge condition, neither forecasts nor intervals can be trusted, which pooled coverage hides.

\emph{Limitations.} The task was changed to forecasting after the first dataset was inspected; the second-life datasets are small; S6 is aged at 50~$^\circ$C and its ``second life'' continues the same protocol; SOH is relative to each cell's first valid measurement; forecasts are conditional on planned usage; and the ablation and every later experiment were decided after the main results (all but the ablation committed before being run). The single-fit regressors see all tasks at once, and the direct literature backbones were run for horizons 1--3 only. The TFT hyperparameters for unseen conditions were tuned with validation rows of all seven S6 conditions, all from one laboratory. Leave-one-condition-out intervals were run for gradient boosting and extrapolation only.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We evaluated a TFT with EWC as a continual learner for forecasting the SOH of second-life cells. Learning a shrunk correction to linear extrapolation made it as accurate as extrapolation and more accurate than gradient boosting on the stream, and more accurate than extrapolation on unseen usage conditions, where gradient boosting with all conditions stored and the same TFT trained jointly were clearly better: at this data scale, learning continually cost more than it gave. Conformal intervals held on unseen conditions only when calibrated on conditions held out from fitting, and not outside the training range; refitting and tracking cut the interval score by 29\%. Future work should test continual learning where earlier data cannot be stored and populations are larger, calibrate intervals across laboratories and chemistries, and add power and safety criteria to the screening.
