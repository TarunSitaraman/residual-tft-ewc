\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{Where the proposed model is strongest.} Extrapolation is accurate when groups arrive in sequence but falls behind on new conditions; gradient boosting is accurate on new conditions but trails in the sequence; Residual TFT-EWC is competitive in both. In the sequence, EWC protects earlier groups when there is something to protect (S6, not S1), cutting forgetting over six horizons by about two thirds. Matching extrapolation is itself hard: fade is smooth and the groups are small (21--27 and 135--225 training samples per task in S1 and S6). On unseen conditions, most of the remaining gap to gradient boosting and to joint training (0.32--0.44~pp) comes from storing earlier data, not from the model: denied that data, gradient boosting is within 0.08~pp there, but on the stream it forgets and falls 0.24--0.46~pp behind, and over 60--64 unseen conditions of S7 and S10 it is 0.8--1.3~pp behind. Where earlier data cannot be kept, for privacy, ownership or volume reasons, continual learning is the natural choice; in our small datasets storing every group was still cheap.

\emph{What drives its accuracy.} Building on the best simple forecaster mattered most. The ablation points to the inputs, not the architecture: diagnostic features hurt the TFT and the calendar time between checks helped, which fits the data: at 50~$^\circ$C, all 20 S6 cells lose more capacity per cycle when checks are further apart. For intervals, the choice of calibration data mattered as much as the model: only errors on conditions left out of training reflect the error on a new one.

\emph{Reproducibility and reuse.} Because significance changed across platforms (Section~\ref{sec:res-repro}), we recommend claiming only differences that replicate and exceed a practical threshold. The reuse classes are a screening aid, not a verdict on suitability: they rest on relative capacity alone, while reuse also depends on power capability, safety, absolute capacity and cost. For a group unlike any seen before, such as the 100\% depth-of-discharge condition, neither the forecasts nor the intervals can be trusted, and pooled coverage hides this.

\emph{Limitations.} We changed the task to forecasting after inspecting the first dataset. The second-life datasets are small; S6 is aged at 50~$^\circ$C and its ``second life'' continues the same test; SOH is relative to each cell's first valid measurement; and forecasts assume the planned usage is known. The ablation and every later experiment were decided after the main results (all but the ablation registered before being run). The TFT settings for unseen conditions were tuned on validation rows of all seven S6 conditions, all from one laboratory. Leave-one-condition-out intervals cover only gradient boosting and extrapolation, and the stream test of gradient boosting refitted on the newest condition was added after the results. The S7 and S10 tests, on first-life data, were added to find a setting where the model wins (the S10 rule after the S7 results).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We proposed Residual TFT-EWC, a continual learner that forecasts the SOH of second-life cells by learning a scaled-down correction to linear extrapolation. Of the models compared, it is the only one that holds up across settings: with groups arriving in sequence it matches extrapolation, beats gradient boosting and forgets far less with EWC, and on unseen usage conditions it beats extrapolation; on all 64 unseen conditions of a new dataset it also beats gradient boosting held to the same rule. Gradient boosting and joint training are more accurate on unseen conditions mainly because they keep every earlier condition's data; without it, gradient boosting is within 0.08~pp on unseen conditions but 0.24--0.46~pp worse on the stream. Forecast intervals held on unseen conditions only when calibrated on conditions left out of training, and not outside the training range. Future work should test the approach where earlier data cannot be stored and groups are larger, and add power and safety criteria to the screening.
