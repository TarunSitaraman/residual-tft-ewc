\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methods}

Given everything measured up to a reference performance test (RPT, or capacity check), we forecast the SOH one to three checks ahead (up to six in follow-up experiments); cells or usage conditions arrive as a stream of tasks, and a model must adapt to each without forgetting the earlier ones. The study first aimed to estimate the \emph{current} SOH from cycle-level features, but on the first dataset these carried almost no health information (median within-cell Spearman correlation with SOH 0.09, against $-0.99$ for elapsed time) and learned estimators were worse than persistence (1.78 and 2.37~pp for gradient boosting and ridge, against 0.99~pp). We therefore reframed the task after seeing the data, committing the forecasting protocol before fitting any forecasting model.

\emph{S1} contains six LG~Chem INR21700-M50T cells that spent 23 months under an EV driving profile~\cite{pozzato2022firstlife} and then about 27 months on peak-shaving duty cycles~\cite{moy2023dutycycles}; each RPT is a C/20 discharge interrupted by C/2 pulses, with impedance spectroscopy (EIS) from the fifth RPT~\cite{moy2024secondlife}. Data-quality findings are handled by fixed rules (e.g.\ RPT~2, run at about 20~$^\circ$C, is dropped).

\emph{S6} was selected by criteria fixed before screening (licence, a capacity-check series, ageing past first-life end of life, heterogeneity that S1 lacks). Its cells were checked every 100 cycles and end at 32--64\% SOH; ``second life'' means continued ageing below 80\% SOH under the same protocol. A check is used only if its C/25 discharge starts from full charge and reaches the cut-off (843 of 847). From the first-life sets \emph{S7--S9}, cells with at least five valid checks are kept (369 cells, 36{,}711 samples).

\subsection{Big-data processing of the ageing cycles}
\label{sec:spark}

Between checks, every S6 cell logged each ageing cycle at about one sample per second: 827 files, 132~GB raw and $9.0\times10^{8}$ rows. Apache Spark~4.2 (PySpark, local mode) reduces them to block-level inputs: one map task per 100-cycle block derives, for every cycle, a validity flag and seven measured responses (end-of-charge and end-of-discharge voltage, constant-voltage hold time and final current, relaxation after charge and after discharge, internal resistance); Spark SQL groups the cached per-cycle table (82{,}529 cycles) by cell and block into the mean, last value and slope of each response (21 inputs), and each block joins the origin at the check that ends it. Charge, energy and current are excluded because the protocol scales them with the previous capacity (within-cell $r=0.97$--$0.999$ with the previous check's capacity). The run takes 20~min on six cores and matches an independent pandas implementation on all 827 blocks (largest difference $2\times10^{-13}$).

\subsection{State of health and forecasting target}
\label{sec:soh}
\label{sec:task}
\label{sec:features}

For cell $b$ with check capacities $C_{b,1}, C_{b,2},\dots$ in time order,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SOH}_{b,k} = 100\,C_{b,k}/C_{b,1}\;\%,
  \label{eq:soh}
\end{equation}
relative to the first valid measurement, not a nominal rating. In S1 the capacity is the charge of the C/20 steps only~\cite[Eq.~(1)]{moy2024secondlife}; the processed tables also counted the pulse charge, shifting some labels by up to 0.09~pp.

A sample is a triple (cell $b$, origin $k$, horizon $h\in\{1,2,3\}$), about one to three months in S1 and 100--300 cycles in S6. The target is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{b,k,h} = \mathrm{SOH}_{b,k+h} - \mathrm{SOH}_{b,k},
  \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
so an error in $\Delta$ equals the error in the future SOH; errors are root-mean-square errors (RMSE) in SOH percentage points (pp). The fade rate $r_{b,k}$ is the least-squares slope of SOH against time $t$ (days or cycles) over the last three valid checks,
\begin{equation}
  r_{b,k} = \frac{\sum_{i=k-2}^{k} (t_{b,i}-\bar t)(\mathrm{SOH}_{b,i}-\overline{\mathrm{SOH}})}{\sum_{i=k-2}^{k} (t_{b,i}-\bar t)^2}.
  \label{eq:fade}
\end{equation}

Inputs use only information available at the origin. \textbf{F0} contains the SOH history ($\mathrm{SOH}_{b,k}$, time or cycles since the first check, $r_{b,k}$), static descriptors (S1: duty cycle and first-life history~\cite{moy2024secondlife}; S6: test conditions) and the \emph{planned} usage until the target (S1: days at each temperature set point and at rest; S6: cycles), so forecasts are conditional on the planned usage. \textbf{F1} adds diagnostics measured at the origin: 47 in S1 (pulse resistances, incremental-capacity and EIS features) and 17 in S6 (plateau charges, incremental-capacity peaks, hysteresis, a resistance proxy). No test current is an input: in S1 the RPT and monthly currents were set from the previous capacity and so encode the label. Missing values are imputed from training data and flagged.

\subsection{Splits and protocols}
\label{sec:protocols}

\emph{Purged chronological splits.} For each cell, cut~A lies at 60\% and cut~B at 80\% of its valid checks. Training samples have their target at or before~A, validation samples their origin at or after~A and target at or before~B, and test samples their origin at or after~B; samples straddling a cut are dropped, so no training label lies after a validation origin and no validation label after a test origin.

\emph{P1, continual stream.} A task is a cell in S1 (six tasks) or a test condition in S6 (seven tasks). At step $i$ of an ordering $\pi$, a strategy receives only task $\pi_i$'s data. Each method runs on 10 orders with 5 seeds, identical across methods.

\emph{P2, unseen group.} One cell (S1) or condition (S6) is held out for testing; models are trained on the others.

\emph{P3, first-life pretraining.} All datasets are mapped onto shared inputs (SOH history, calendar and cycle time, planned time to the target, operating conditions and chemistry; undefined conditions flagged as missing). Models are pretrained on the pool, which also gives the input scaling and the first EWC anchor; the seven S6 conditions then follow as a stream.

\subsection{Models}
\label{sec:backbones}

Preprocessing (standardisation, median imputation with missing indicators, target scaling) is fitted on the first task and then frozen. The \emph{MLP} has hidden layers of 128 and 64 ReLU units (dropout 0.1). The proposed backbone adapts the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT)~\cite{lim2021tft}: a window of the $W{=}4$ most recent origins of the same cell and horizon (inputs only, never labels) passes through per-variable embeddings, variable selection by gated residual networks, an LSTM encoder~\cite{hochreiter1997lstm} and interpretable two-head attention masked to past steps (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}; $d{=}8$, 12{,}708 parameters with the shared inputs).

\input{figures/proposed_model}

\emph{Strategies.} Fine-tuning trains on the current task only (lower bound); experience replay~\cite{rolnick2019replay} stores $B$ samples per finished task; joint training fits all tasks at once (upper bound); and Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)~\cite{kirkpatrick2017ewc} adds, for each finished task $j$,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_{p} F_{j,p}\,\big(\theta_p - \theta^{*}_{j,p}\big)^2,
  \label{eq:ewc}
\end{equation}
with $\theta^*_j$ the parameters after task $j$ and $F_j$ the empirical diagonal Fisher information over up to 500 training samples. \emph{TFT\,+\,EWC} is the proposed model. Training uses Adam~\cite{kingma2015adam}, batches of 64, at most 200 epochs and early stopping on the current task's validation RMSE.

\emph{Reference forecasters} are persistence ($\hat\Delta=0$), linear extrapolation $\hat\Delta_{b,k,h} = r_{b,k}(t_{b,k+h}-t_{b,k})$, ridge regression (on planned usage only, F0 or F1) and histogram gradient boosting on F1.

\emph{Learned residual.} Because linear extrapolation proved hardest to beat, a pre-registered follow-up trains the continual learners on the correction to it. With $\hat\Delta^{\mathrm{lin}}_{b,k,h}$ the extrapolation above (zero when $r_{b,k}$ is missing), the model $g_\theta$ learns $\Delta_{b,k,h}-\hat\Delta^{\mathrm{lin}}_{b,k,h}$ and the forecast is
\begin{equation}
  \hat\Delta_{b,k,h} = \hat\Delta^{\mathrm{lin}}_{b,k,h} + g_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{b,k,h}).
  \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
The residual and the direct (target $\Delta$) forms are compared on the S6 stream with the P3 shared inputs, from scratch, for horizons 1--3 (E1) and 1--6 (E2).

\emph{Shift-adaptive EWC and literature backbones.} A second pre-registered experiment lets the EWC strength follow the shift between groups (shift-adaptive EWC, SA-EWC): when task $t$ starts, the penalty~\eqref{eq:ewc} of each finished task $j$ is multiplied by $d_{j,t}=\big((\lVert\mu_t-\mu_j\rVert^2+\lVert\sigma_t-\sigma_j\rVert^2)/D\big)^{1/2}$, the per-dimension 2-Wasserstein distance between diagonal Gaussians fitted to the two tasks' preprocessed training inputs, with $\lambda_0$ tuned as $\lambda$. BiLSTM~\cite{ref10}, TCN~\cite{ref8,ref10} and Transformer-encoder continual learners (TFT input window, within 20\% of its parameters) and single-fit GPR~\cite{ref3,ref4}, random forest and SVR are run in residual and direct form.

\emph{Shrinkage and forecast intervals.} A third pre-registered experiment calibrates the residual model after training, leaving its weights unchanged. Per horizon $h$, the learned correction is shrunk towards extrapolation by $\alpha_h=\min\!\big(1,\max(0,\sum p\,y/\sum p^2)\big)$, with $p$ the predicted and $y$ the observed correction on calibration rows. A split-conformal interval~\cite{lei2018conformal} $\hat\Delta\pm q_h$ takes as $q_h$ the $\lceil (n+1)(1-a)\rceil$-th smallest of the $n$ absolute calibration residuals at horizon $h$. Intervals at level $1-a$ (90\% and 80\%) are scored by coverage, width and the interval score~\cite{gneiting2007scoring} $2q_h + \tfrac{2}{a}\max(|\Delta-\hat\Delta|-q_h,0)$ (pp; lower is better). On the stream both are refitted after each task on its validation rows; extrapolation gets intervals the same way.

\emph{Unseen conditions.} A fourth experiment repeats P2 on S6 for the shrunk residual TFT\,+\,EWC, streamed over the six training conditions in the ten evaluation orders with the P1 tuned values; shrinkage and intervals are fitted once on their pooled validation rows. Origins with a fade rate (H$^+$) and without (H$^-$, a cell's first two checks, where extrapolation is persistence) are reported separately. A fifth experiment calibrates intervals leaving one condition out: each training condition is predicted by a model fitted on the other five, and the absolute residuals at all its origins, pooled over the six, give $q_h$ (cross-conformal~\cite{barber2021jackknife}, optionally per horizon and stratum). Its forecaster is gradient boosting, the most accurate in P2; being deterministic, it must replicate across both horizon sets instead of seed sets. In a sixth the held-out condition arrives over time: as each target is measured, gradient boosting is refitted with it and the half-width moves by $c\,q_h(e-\alpha)$, $e=1$ for a miss (quantile tracking~\cite{angelopoulos2023pid}; $c$ chosen on the training conditions).

\subsection{Hyperparameters, metrics and statistics}
\label{sec:tuning}
\label{sec:metrics}

Hyperparameters are chosen on validation data from tuning orders disjoint from the evaluation orders: the learning rate per backbone ($\{3\cdot10^{-4},\dots,10^{-1}\}$; lowest validation plasticity of fine-tuning streams), then replay $B\in\{50,\dots,400\}$ and EWC $\lambda\in\{10^0,\dots,10^5\}$; a grid whose best value is at an edge is extended by two values. From P3 on, ten tuning orders and the one-standard-error rule (the smallest grid value within one standard error of the best) are used.

After each step $i$, every task's test set is evaluated, giving $R_{i,j} = \mathrm{RMSE}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{te}}_{\pi_j}\mid\theta_i)$. Following~\cite{lopezpaz2017gem,chaudhry2018rwalk}, we report the final average error $\tfrac{1}{T}\sum_{j} R_{T,j}$, backward transfer $\tfrac{1}{T-1}\sum_{j<T} (R_{T,j} - R_{j,j})$ and forgetting $\tfrac{1}{T-1}\sum_{j<T} (R_{T,j} - \min_{j\le i<T} R_{i,j})$ (positive values mean forgetting), the skill $1-\mathrm{RMSE}/\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{persistence}}$, and the agreement of the reuse class of the forecast SOH with the measured class (C0 $>95\%$, C1 90--95\%, C2 80--90\%, C3 $\le80\%$, fixed in advance).

For P1 and P2 the same predictions are also scored with further regression metrics (MAE, median error, bias, $R^2_\Delta$ on the change since the origin, which is $\le0$ for persistence, and others) and, on all held-out samples pooled, reuse-class metrics (accuracy, macro-F1, the Matthews correlation coefficient, MCC, and others) and the AUC of the decision SOH $\le80\%$ (end of first life); the full sets are in the repository (decision D30).
Means carry 95\% bootstrap intervals~\cite{efron1993bootstrap}. Methods are compared by two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests~\cite{wilcoxon1945} on matched runs or held-out groups, with rank-biserial effect sizes~\cite{kerby2014rankbiserial} and Holm correction~\cite{holm1979}. A difference $d$ is \emph{replicated} only if it is significant in the main seed set (0--4) and in a replicate set (5--9), with the same sign and $|d|\ge 0.05$~pp in both; $|d|<0.05$~pp in both means practically equivalent. The threshold is about half the label shift caused by the pulse-charge definition.

\subsection{Implementation and availability}
\label{sec:repro}

The pipeline uses Python~3.11, NumPy~2.4, pandas~3.0, scikit-learn~1.9 and PyTorch~2.14.1 (P1 and P2 were first run on an Intel Xeon with an unrecorded PyTorch version and re-run on an Intel Core~5~210H), with one CPU thread and seeded, deterministic algorithms. Every result file records configuration, seeds, library versions and the Git commit; unit tests cover input causality, split purging and label exclusion from the inputs. The datasets are at doi:10.17605/OSF.IO/8JNR5 (S1), 10.57745/OLBXKT (S6), 10.35097/1969 (S7), 10.5281/zenodo.10963339 (S8) and 10.5281/zenodo.6405084 (S9); code, decision log and results are kept in a private repository and are available from the authors on request.
