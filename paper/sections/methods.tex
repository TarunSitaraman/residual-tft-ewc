\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methods}

We forecast the SOH one to three checks ahead (up to six in later experiments) from everything measured up to a reference performance test (RPT), i.e.\ a capacity check, with cells or usage conditions arriving one after another as tasks. The study first set out to estimate the \emph{current} SOH. On the first dataset, however, cycle-level features carried almost no health information (median within-cell Spearman correlation with SOH 0.09, against $-0.99$ for elapsed time), and learned estimators did worse than persistence, which simply repeats the last measured SOH (1.78 and 2.37~pp for gradient boosting and ridge, against 0.99~pp). We therefore changed the task after seeing the data, fixing the forecasting protocol before fitting any forecaster.

\emph{S1} contains six LG~Chem INR21700-M50T cells that spent 23 months under an EV driving profile~\cite{pozzato2022firstlife} and then about 27 months on peak-shaving duty cycles~\cite{moy2023dutycycles}; each RPT is a C/20 discharge interrupted by C/2 pulses, with impedance spectroscopy (EIS) from the fifth RPT~\cite{moy2024secondlife}. Data-quality findings are handled by fixed rules (e.g.\ RPT~2, run at about 20~$^\circ$C, is dropped).

\emph{S6} was chosen by criteria fixed before the search (licence, a series of capacity checks, ageing past the end of first life, and the variety that S1 lacks). Its cells were checked every 100 cycles and end at 32--64\% SOH; here ``second life'' means further ageing below 80\% SOH under the same test. A check is used only if its C/25 discharge starts from full charge and reaches the cut-off voltage (843 of 847). From the first-life sets \emph{S7--S9} we keep cells with at least five valid checks (369 cells, 36{,}711 samples). \emph{S10} uses the discharge capacity of each 23~$^\circ$C check-up (2{,}587 of 2{,}766 kept).

\subsection{Big-data processing of the ageing cycles}
\label{sec:spark}

Between checks, every S6 cell logged each ageing cycle about once per second (827 files, 132~GB, $9.0\times10^{8}$ rows). Apache Spark~4.2 (PySpark, local mode) condenses them into the mean, last value and slope of seven measured quantities per 100-cycle block (21 inputs), each linked to the check that ends the block (details and validation in the supplement).

\subsection{State of health and forecasting target}
\label{sec:soh}
\label{sec:task}
\label{sec:features}

For cell $b$ with check capacities $C_{b,1}, C_{b,2},\dots$ in time order,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SOH}_{b,k} = 100\,C_{b,k}/C_{b,1}\;\%,
  \label{eq:soh}
\end{equation}
relative to the first valid measurement, not to the rated capacity. In S1 the capacity is the charge of the C/20 steps only~\cite[Eq.~(1)]{moy2024secondlife}; the processed tables also counted the pulse charge, shifting some labels by up to 0.09~pp.

A sample is a triple (cell $b$, origin $k$, horizon $h\in\{1,2,3\}$): a forecast made at check $k$ for check $k+h$, about one to three months ahead in S1 and 100--300 cycles in S6. The target is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{b,k,h} = \mathrm{SOH}_{b,k+h} - \mathrm{SOH}_{b,k},
  \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
so an error in $\Delta$ is the same as the error in the future SOH. Errors are root-mean-square errors (RMSE) in SOH percentage points (pp). The fade rate $r_{b,k}$ is the least-squares slope of SOH against time $t$ (days or cycles) over the last three valid checks,
\begin{equation}
  r_{b,k} = \frac{\sum_{i=k-2}^{k} (t_{b,i}-\bar t)(\mathrm{SOH}_{b,i}-\overline{\mathrm{SOH}})}{\sum_{i=k-2}^{k} (t_{b,i}-\bar t)^2}.
  \label{eq:fade}
\end{equation}

Inputs use only what is known at the origin. \textbf{F0} contains the SOH history ($\mathrm{SOH}_{b,k}$, time or cycles since the first check, $r_{b,k}$), fixed descriptors (S1: duty cycle and first-life history~\cite{moy2024secondlife}; S6: test conditions) and the \emph{planned} usage until the target (S1: days at each temperature set point and at rest; S6: cycles), so forecasts assume the planned usage is known. \textbf{F1} adds diagnostics measured at the origin: 47 in S1 (pulse resistances, incremental-capacity and EIS features) and 17 in S6 (plateau charges, incremental-capacity peaks, hysteresis, a resistance proxy). No test current is used as an input: in S1 the RPT and monthly currents were set from the previous capacity, so they would give away the label. Missing values are filled in from training data and flagged.

\subsection{Splits and protocols}
\label{sec:protocols}

\emph{Purged chronological splits.} Each cell's checks are cut at 60\% (A) and 80\% (B). Training targets lie at or before~A; validation samples start at or after~A and end by~B; test samples start at or after~B. Samples that straddle a cut are dropped, so no training label falls in the validation period and no validation label in the test period.

\emph{P1, continual stream.} A task is one cell in S1 (six tasks) or one test condition in S6 (seven tasks). At step $i$ of an ordering $\pi$, a method sees only the data of task $\pi_i$. Every method runs on the same 10 task orders with 5 seeds.

\emph{P2, unseen group.} One cell (S1) or condition (S6) is held out for testing, and models are trained on the rest.

\emph{P3, first-life pretraining.} All datasets are mapped onto shared inputs (SOH history, calendar and cycle time, planned time to the target, operating conditions and chemistry; undefined conditions flagged as missing). Models are first trained on the pool, which also sets the input scaling and the first EWC anchor; the seven S6 conditions then follow as a stream.

\subsection{Models}
\label{sec:backbones}

Preprocessing (standardisation, median imputation with missing indicators, target scaling) is fitted on the first task and then frozen. The \emph{MLP} has hidden layers of 128 and 64 ReLU units (dropout 0.1). The proposed model builds on the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT)~\cite{lim2021tft}: it looks at the $W{=}4$ most recent origins of the same cell and horizon (inputs only, never labels), which pass through per-variable embeddings, variable selection by gated residual networks, an LSTM encoder~\cite{hochreiter1997lstm} and two-head attention restricted to past steps (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}; $d{=}8$, 12{,}708 parameters with the shared inputs).

\input{figures/proposed_model}

\emph{Strategies.} Fine-tuning trains on the current task only (a lower bound); experience replay~\cite{rolnick2019replay} keeps $B$ samples of each finished task; joint training fits all tasks at once (an upper bound); and Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)~\cite{kirkpatrick2017ewc} penalises changes to the weights that mattered for each finished task $j$,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_{p} F_{j,p}\,\big(\theta_p - \theta^{*}_{j,p}\big)^2,
  \label{eq:ewc}
\end{equation}
where $\theta^*_j$ are the parameters after task $j$ and $F_j$ is the empirical diagonal Fisher information over up to 500 training samples. \emph{TFT\,+\,EWC} is the proposed continual learner; trained on the residual~\eqref{eq:residual} below, with shrinkage and intervals, it becomes the proposed \emph{Residual TFT-EWC} (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}). Training uses Adam~\cite{kingma2015adam}, batches of 64, at most 200 epochs and early stopping on the current task's validation RMSE.

\emph{Reference forecasters} are persistence ($\hat\Delta=0$), linear extrapolation of the fade rate, $\hat\Delta_{b,k,h} = r_{b,k}(t_{b,k+h}-t_{b,k})$, ridge regression (on planned usage only, F0 or F1) and histogram gradient boosting on F1.

\emph{Learned residual.} Because linear extrapolation proved hardest to beat, a follow-up experiment, registered in advance, trains the continual learners to correct it rather than replace it. With $\hat\Delta^{\mathrm{lin}}_{b,k,h}$ the extrapolation above (zero when $r_{b,k}$ is missing), the model $g_\theta$ learns $\Delta_{b,k,h}-\hat\Delta^{\mathrm{lin}}_{b,k,h}$ and the forecast is
\begin{equation}
  \hat\Delta_{b,k,h} = \hat\Delta^{\mathrm{lin}}_{b,k,h} + g_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{b,k,h}).
  \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
The residual and the direct (target $\Delta$) forms are compared on the S6 stream with the P3 shared inputs, from scratch, for horizons 1--3 (E1) and 1--6 (E2).

\emph{Shift-adaptive EWC and literature backbones.} A second registered experiment scales each finished task's EWC penalty by the input shift between tasks (SA-EWC) and compares BiLSTM~\cite{ref10}, TCN~\cite{ref8,ref10} and Transformer continual learners and GPR~\cite{ref3,ref4}, random forest and SVR fitted once (definitions in the supplement).

\emph{Shrinkage and forecast intervals.} A third registered experiment calibrates the residual model after training, without changing its weights. For each horizon $h$, the learned correction is scaled back towards extrapolation by $\alpha_h=\min\!\big(1,\max(0,\sum p\,y/\sum p^2)\big)$, with $p$ the predicted and $y$ the observed correction on calibration rows. A split-conformal interval~\cite{lei2018conformal} $\hat\Delta\pm q_h$ takes as $q_h$ the $\lceil (n+1)(1-a)\rceil$-th smallest of the $n$ absolute calibration errors at horizon $h$. Intervals at level $1-a$ (90\% and 80\%) are scored by coverage, width and the interval score~\cite{gneiting2007scoring} $2q_h + \tfrac{2}{a}\max(|\Delta-\hat\Delta|-q_h,0)$ (pp; lower is better). On the stream both are refitted after each task on its validation rows; extrapolation gets intervals the same way.

\emph{Unseen conditions.} A fourth experiment repeats P2 on S6 for the shrunk residual TFT\,+\,EWC, streamed over the six training conditions in the ten evaluation orders with the P1 tuned values; shrinkage and intervals are fitted once on their pooled validation rows. Origins with a fade rate (H$^+$) and without one (H$^-$, a cell's first two checks, where extrapolation equals persistence) are reported separately. A fifth experiment calibrates gradient-boosting intervals by leaving one condition out: each training condition is predicted by a model fitted on the other five, and their pooled absolute errors give $q_h$ (the cross-validation interval of~\cite{barber2021jackknife} with conditions as folds; without a finite-sample guarantee, coverage is measured on held-out conditions, and as the forecaster is deterministic, results must replicate across both horizon sets).

\emph{Many unseen conditions.} Two further registered experiments repeat P2 on S7 (67 conditions) and S10 (71): seven conditions tune the hyperparameters and the rest (60, 64) form five test folds. The residual TFT\,+\,EWC is streamed over each fold's training conditions and compared, by Wilcoxon tests over held-out conditions, with gradient boosting refitted on each stream's last condition, gradient boosting on all conditions and extrapolation; a question counts only if replicated for horizons 1--3 and 1--6. S7 shrinks at every origin, S10 only at H$^+$, a rule fixed after the S7 results.

\subsection{Hyperparameters, metrics and statistics}
\label{sec:tuning}
\label{sec:metrics}

Hyperparameters are chosen on validation data from tuning orders disjoint from the evaluation orders: the learning rate per backbone ($3\cdot10^{-4}$ to $10^{-1}$), then replay $B$ (50--400) and EWC $\lambda$ ($10^0$--$10^5$), extending any grid whose best value lies at an edge. From P3 on, ten tuning orders and the one-standard-error rule (the smallest grid value within one standard error of the best) are used.

After each step $i$, every task's test set is evaluated, giving $R_{i,j} = \mathrm{RMSE}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{te}}_{\pi_j}\mid\theta_i)$. Following~\cite{lopezpaz2017gem,chaudhry2018rwalk}, we report the final average error $\tfrac{1}{T}\sum_{j} R_{T,j}$, backward transfer $\tfrac{1}{T-1}\sum_{j<T} (R_{T,j} - R_{j,j})$ and forgetting $\tfrac{1}{T-1}\sum_{j<T} (R_{T,j} - \min_{j\le i<T} R_{i,j})$ (positive values mean forgetting), the skill $1-\mathrm{RMSE}/\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{persistence}}$, and the agreement of the reuse class of the forecast SOH with the measured class (C0 $>95\%$, C1 90--95\%, C2 80--90\%, C3 $\le80\%$, fixed in advance). The classes screen for capacity fade relative to the first valid check; they do not by themselves show suitability for an application.

P2 also reports MSE, MAE, MedAE, bias, MAPE, NRMSE and $R^2$ (and $R^2_\Delta$ of the change, ${\le}0$ for persistence); for the reuse class, accuracy, balanced accuracy, macro precision, recall, specificity and F1, MCC, Cohen's $\kappa$ and $\kappa_w$; and the AUC of SOH${\le}80\%$ (Tables SI--SII of the supplement). Point forecasts admit no log loss.
Means carry 95\% bootstrap intervals~\cite{efron1993bootstrap}. Methods are compared by two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests~\cite{wilcoxon1945} on matched runs or held-out groups, with rank-biserial effect sizes~\cite{kerby2014rankbiserial} and Holm correction~\cite{holm1979}. A difference $d$ is \emph{replicated} only if it is significant in the main seed set (0--4) and in a replicate set (5--9), with the same sign and $|d|\ge 0.05$~pp in both; $|d|<0.05$~pp in both means practically equivalent. The threshold is about half the label shift caused by the pulse-charge definition.

\subsection{Implementation and availability}
\label{sec:repro}

The pipeline uses Python~3.11, NumPy~2.4, pandas~3.0, scikit-learn~1.9 and PyTorch~2.14.1 (P1 and P2 were first run on an Intel Xeon with an unrecorded PyTorch version and re-run on an Intel Core~5~210H), with one CPU thread and seeded, deterministic algorithms. Every result file records configuration, seeds, library versions and the Git commit, and unit tests cover input causality, split purging and label exclusion. The datasets are at doi:10.17605/OSF.IO/8JNR5 (S1), 10.57745/OLBXKT (S6), 10.35097/1969 (S7), 10.5281/zenodo.10963339 (S8), 10.5281/zenodo.6405084 (S9) and 10.6084/m9.figshare.25975315 (S10). Code, decision log, pre-registrations and results: \url{https://github.com/TarunSitaraman/continual-learning-heterogeneous-second-life-ev-batteries}.
