\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methods}

Given everything measured up to a reference performance test (RPT, or capacity check), we forecast the SOH one to three checks ahead. Cells or usage conditions arrive as a stream of tasks, and a model must adapt to each new task without forgetting the earlier ones. Fig.~\ref{fig:framework} summarises the framework.

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.86\textwidth]{figures/framework.png}
  \caption{Framework of the study. Public data are verified and audited, converted into forecasting samples with purged chronological splits, and evaluated under three protocols with continual-learning metrics, a replication rule and a reuse-class mapping.}
  \label{fig:framework}
\end{figure*}

\emph{Change of task after inspecting the data.} The study was first designed to estimate the \emph{current} SOH from cycle-level features. On the first dataset these features carried almost no health information (median within-cell Spearman correlation with SOH 0.09, against $-0.99$ for elapsed time), and learned estimators were worse than persistence (1.78 and 2.37~pp for gradient boosting and ridge regression, against 0.99~pp). We therefore reframed the task as forecasting, the Temporal Fusion Transformer's native setting. This decision was taken after seeing the data; the forecasting protocol (features, splits, methods, grids and tests) was then committed to the repository before any forecasting model was fitted, and deviations are reported.

\emph{S1} (Stanford) contains six LG~Chem INR21700-M50T cells that spent 23 months under an EV driving profile with different charge rates~\cite{pozzato2022firstlife} and then about 27 months on synthetic peak-shaving duty cycles~\cite{moy2023dutycycles}; each RPT is a C/20 discharge interrupted by C/2 pulses at three voltages, with impedance spectroscopy (EIS) from the fifth RPT~\cite{moy2024secondlife}. Its heterogeneity lies in first-life ageing and duty cycle among cells of one type. Data-quality findings are handled by fixed rules: RPT~2 (run at about 20~$^\circ$C) is excluded, off-protocol pulses and galvanostatic spectra are set to missing, and RPT~7's aggregate EIS files are decoded and checked against the authors' values.

\emph{S6} (Wheeler \textit{et al.}) was selected by criteria fixed before screening: a reuse licence, a series of capacity checks, ageing past first-life end of life, heterogeneity that S1 lacks and verifiable metadata. Its cells were characterised every 100 cycles and end at 32--64\% SOH; ``second life'' here means continued ageing below 80\% SOH under the same protocol. A check is used only if its C/25 discharge starts from full charge and reaches the cut-off (843 of 847 checks).

\emph{S7--S9} are first-life datasets used for pretraining. Cells with at least five valid checks are kept: 369 cells and 36{,}711 samples.

\subsection{State of health and forecasting target}
\label{sec:soh}
\label{sec:task}
\label{sec:features}

For cell $b$ with check capacities $C_{b,1}, C_{b,2},\dots$ in time order,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SOH}_{b,k} = 100\,C_{b,k}/C_{b,1}\;\%,
  \label{eq:soh}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ relative to the first valid measurement, not to a nominal rating; for S1 this is already an aged state. In S1 the capacity is the charge of the C/20 steps only~\cite[Eq.~(1)]{moy2024secondlife}. The processed tables we started from also counted the pulse charge, which shifts some labels by up to 0.09~pp, comparable to one RPT's fade.

A sample is a triple (cell $b$, origin $k$, horizon $h\in\{1,2,3\}$), about one to three months in S1 and 100--300 cycles in S6. The target is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{b,k,h} = \mathrm{SOH}_{b,k+h} - \mathrm{SOH}_{b,k},
  \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
so an error in $\Delta$ equals the error in the future SOH; errors are in SOH percentage points (pp). Over a set $\mathcal{D}$ of $N$ samples,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{RMSE}(\mathcal{D}) = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_{\mathcal{D}} \big(\hat\Delta_{b,k,h} - \Delta_{b,k,h}\big)^2}.
  \label{eq:rmse}
\end{equation}
The fade rate $r_{b,k}$ is the least-squares slope of SOH against time $t$ (days or cycles) over the last three valid checks,
\begin{equation}
  r_{b,k} = \frac{\sum_{i=k-2}^{k} (t_{b,i}-\bar t)(\mathrm{SOH}_{b,i}-\overline{\mathrm{SOH}})}{\sum_{i=k-2}^{k} (t_{b,i}-\bar t)^2}.
  \label{eq:fade}
\end{equation}

Inputs use only information available at the origin. \textbf{F0} contains the SOH history ($\mathrm{SOH}_{b,k}$, time or cycles since the first check, $r_{b,k}$), static descriptors (S1: duty cycle and first-life history~\cite{moy2024secondlife}; S6: test conditions) and the \emph{planned} usage until the target (S1: days at each temperature set point and at rest; S6: cycles), so forecasts are conditional on the planned usage. \textbf{F1} adds diagnostics measured at the origin: 47 in S1 (pulse resistances, charge between pulse voltages, incremental-capacity peaks of the C/20 discharge and EIS features) and 17 in S6 (plateau charges, incremental-capacity peaks, hysteresis, a resistance proxy and capacity ratios). No test current is an input: in S1 the RPT and monthly discharge currents were set from the previous capacity and therefore encode the label. Missing values are imputed from training data and flagged.

\subsection{Splits and protocols}
\label{sec:protocols}

\emph{Purged chronological splits.} For each cell, cut~A lies at 60\% and cut~B at 80\% of its valid checks. Training samples have their target at or before~A, validation samples their origin at or after~A and target at or before~B, and test samples their origin at or after~B; samples straddling a cut are dropped, so no training label lies after a validation origin and no validation label after a test origin.

\emph{P1, continual stream.} A task is a cell in S1 (six tasks) or a test condition in S6 (seven tasks). At step $i$ of an ordering $\pi$, a strategy receives only task $\pi_i$'s data. Each method runs on 10 orders with 5 seeds, identical across methods.

\emph{P2, unseen group.} One cell (S1) or condition (S6) is held out; models are trained on the others and tested on all its samples.

\emph{P3, first-life pretraining.} All datasets are mapped onto shared inputs: SOH history, calendar and cycle time, planned time to the target and operating conditions (temperature, charge and discharge rates, depth of discharge, charge voltage and CV phase, storage SoC, chemistry), with undefined conditions flagged as missing. Models are pretrained on the pool, which also supplies the input scaling and the first EWC anchor; the seven S6 conditions then follow as a stream. We compare training from scratch, pretraining with fine-tuning and pretraining with EWC for both backbones, and record the pool test error before and after the stream.

\subsection{Models}
\label{sec:backbones}

Preprocessing (standardisation, median imputation with missing indicators, target scaling) is fitted on the first task and then frozen. The \emph{MLP} has hidden layers of 128 and 64 ReLU units (dropout 0.1). The proposed backbone adapts the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT)~\cite{lim2021tft}: a window of the $W{=}4$ most recent origins of the same cell and horizon (inputs only, never labels) passes through per-variable embeddings, variable selection by gated residual networks, an LSTM encoder~\cite{hochreiter1997lstm} and interpretable two-head attention masked to past steps.

\emph{Strategies.} Fine-tuning trains on the current task only (lower bound); experience replay~\cite{rolnick2019replay} stores $B$ samples per finished task; joint training fits all tasks at once (upper bound); and Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)~\cite{kirkpatrick2017ewc} adds, for each finished task $j$,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_{p} F_{j,p}\,\big(\theta_p - \theta^{*}_{j,p}\big)^2,
  \label{eq:ewc}
\end{equation}
with $\theta^*_j$ the parameters after task $j$ and $F_j$ the empirical diagonal Fisher information over up to 500 training samples. \emph{TFT\,+\,EWC} is the proposed model. Training uses Adam~\cite{kingma2015adam}, batches of 64, at most 200 epochs and early stopping on the current task's validation RMSE.

\emph{Reference forecasters} are persistence ($\hat\Delta=0$), linear extrapolation $\hat\Delta_{b,k,h} = r_{b,k}(t_{b,k+h}-t_{b,k})$, ridge regression (on planned usage only, F0 or F1) and histogram gradient boosting on F1.

\subsection{Hyperparameters, metrics and statistics}
\label{sec:tuning}
\label{sec:metrics}

Hyperparameters are chosen on validation data from tuning orders disjoint from the evaluation orders: the learning rate per backbone (lowest validation plasticity $\tfrac{1}{T}\sum_j R_{j,j}$ of fine-tuning streams), then replay $B\in\{50,\dots,400\}$ and EWC $\lambda\in\{10^0,\dots,10^5\}$; a selection at a grid edge extends the grid. For P3, ten tuning orders are used with the one-standard-error rule
\begin{equation}
  g^{\star} = \min\big\{\,g : \bar m_g \le \bar m_{\hat g} + s_{\hat g}\big\},\quad \hat g = \arg\min_g \bar m_g,
  \label{eq:onese}
\end{equation}
where $\bar m_g$ is the mean validation criterion of grid value $g$ and $s_g$ its standard error.

After each step $i$, every task's test set is evaluated, giving $R_{i,j} = \mathrm{RMSE}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{te}}_{\pi_j}\mid\theta_i)$. Following~\cite{lopezpaz2017gem,chaudhry2018rwalk}, we report the final average error $\tfrac{1}{T}\sum_{j} R_{T,j}$, backward transfer $\tfrac{1}{T-1}\sum_{j<T} (R_{T,j} - R_{j,j})$ and forgetting $\tfrac{1}{T-1}\sum_{j<T} (R_{T,j} - \min_{j\le i<T} R_{i,j})$ (positive values mean forgetting), the skill $1-\mathrm{RMSE}/\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{persistence}}$, and the agreement of the reuse class of the forecast SOH with the measured class (C0 $>95\%$, C1 90--95\%, C2 80--90\%, C3 $\le80\%$, fixed in advance).

Means carry 95\% bootstrap intervals~\cite{efron1993bootstrap}. Methods are compared by two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests~\cite{wilcoxon1945} on matched runs or held-out groups, with rank-biserial effect sizes~\cite{kerby2014rankbiserial} and Holm correction~\cite{holm1979}. A difference $d$ is \emph{replicated} only if it is significant in the main seed set (0--4) and in a replicate set (5--9), with the same sign and $|d|\ge 0.05$~pp in both; $|d|<0.05$~pp in both means practically equivalent. The threshold is about half the label shift caused by the pulse-charge definition.

\subsection{Implementation and availability}
\label{sec:repro}

The pipeline uses Python~3.11, NumPy~2.4, pandas~3.0, scikit-learn~1.9 and PyTorch. P1 and P2 were first run on an Intel Xeon (PyTorch version not recorded) and re-run, like P3 and the ablation, with PyTorch~2.14.1 on an Intel Core~5~210H, with one CPU thread and seeded, deterministic algorithms. Every result file records configuration, seeds, library versions and the Git commit. Unit tests cover input causality, split purging and the exclusion of labels and protocol currents from the inputs. The datasets are at doi:10.17605/OSF.IO/8JNR5 (S1), 10.57745/OLBXKT (S6), 10.35097/1969 (S7), 10.5281/zenodo.10963339 (S8) and 10.5281/zenodo.6405084 (S9); code, decision log and results are at \url{https://github.com/TarunSitaraman/continual-learning-heterogeneous-second-life-ev-batteries}. \todo{Confirm repository visibility and code licence before submission.}
